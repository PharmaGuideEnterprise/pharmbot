### Introduction

The following is a summary of the use of analgesics, anti-infectives, anesthetics and medical emergency drugs in dentistry. This information is not intended to present a comprehensive review; the reader is therefore encouraged to seek additional and confirmatory information.

### Analgesics

An algorithm for the management of acute postoperative dental pain in adults is shown in [Figure 1](#ci704n00105).

Consider the following points in the use of analgesics:[[1]](#ci704n00117)[[2]](#DowdFJJohnsonBSMariottiAJEds.Pharma-9F9691AF)

- Eliminate the source of pain, if possible
- Individualize regimens
- Optimize dose and frequency before switching to another medication
- Consider a loading dose and/or a preoperative dose for nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs)[[2]](#DowdFJJohnsonBSMariottiAJEds.Pharma-9F9691AF)[[3]](#SavageMGHenryMA.PreoperativeNonster-9F967C1B)
- Avoid chronic use of any analgesic
- Reduce the dose of NSAIDs in older individuals or avoid completely. Acetaminophen is the analgesic of choice in the elderly patient[[4]](#refitem-17212-792BDE0D)
- Postoperative pain is most significant during the first 3 days; providing a 3-day supply of analgesics should be sufficient
- Maximize nonopioid use before adding an opioid (opioids are usually not required)
- **Do not add opioids unless the nonopioid is ineffective**
- Limit the prescribing of opioids to the minimum amount possible
- Examples of nonopioid regimens include:

  - acetaminophen 1000 mg Q4H (to a maximum of 4000 mg per day)
  - ibuprofen 600 mg QID
  - combination of acetaminophen 1000 mg with ibuprofen 400 mg Q4?6H PRN pain
- The combination of high-dose acetaminophen and an NSAID may increase the risk of renal toxicity; use caution in predisposed patients (e.g., elderly, pre-existing renal disease)[[5]](#ForedCMEjerbladELindbladPEtAl.Aceta-9F97786D)

Be aware of the contraindications and cautions for NSAIDs, including ASA (see also individual product monographs and CPhA monographs as well as comprehensive drug interaction references).

Avoid **NSAIDs** in patients with:[[6]](#ci704n00115)

- History of allergic reaction to any NSAID, including ASA (celecoxib may be considered, depending on the nature of the reaction[[7]](#KowalskiMLAseroRBavbekSEtAl.Classif-9FA5F3F1))
- ASA-induced asthma and nasal polyps (celecoxib may be considered, depending on the degree of asthma control[[7]](#KowalskiMLAseroRBavbekSEtAl.Classif-9FA5F3F1))
- Inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn disease, ulcerative colitis)
- Renal disease
- History of bleeding disorder or concurrent use of anticoagulants
- Concurrent use of antineoplastic doses of methotrexate
- Concurrent use of lithium
- Concurrent use of digoxin in older individuals

Avoid or use **NSAIDs** with caution, for the shortest possible time, in the elderly or patients with:

- History of myocardial infarction or stroke[[8]](#ci704n00160)[[9]](#ci704n00161)
- Concurrent use of ACE inhibitors, loop diuretics or beta-blockers, particularly in patients with heart failure (avoid NSAIDs or limit use to ?4 days)
- Pregnancy: avoid in third trimester because NSAIDs may cause constriction of the fetal ductus arteriosus. NSAIDs are not recommended after 20 weeks? gestation due to increased risk of oligohydramnios.[[10]](#HealthCanada.UseOfNon-steroidalAnti) If NSAIDs are necessary, prescribe the lowest effective dose and for the shortest duration possible. Use beyond 48 hours may require ultrasound monitoring of amniotic fluid. NSAIDs should be discontinued if fetal oligohydramnios is detected

Be aware of the contraindications and cautions for **opioids**; reduce dose or avoid completely for the following:

- Elderly
- Severe respiratory disease
- Severe inflammatory bowel disease
- Concurrent use of alcohol, cannabis or other CNS depressants
- Breastfeeding patient: use codeine with caution; choose the lowest effective dose and limit treatment to less than 3?4 days. Patients with an ultra-rapid metabolizer CYP2D6 genotype may experience intensified effects, and resultant high levels of morphine in maternal serum could cause relatively high morphine levels in breast milk.[[11]](#ci704n00170) A discussion of the use of opioids in this population can be found in Drug Use during Breastfeeding

The Royal College of Dental Surgeons of Ontario recommends:[[12]](#refitem-15216-69BEAC19)

- **An opioid is required only in a minority of situations**
- Before prescribing an opioid for any patient, consider the following questions:

  - Is the patient?s pain well documented?
  - Has nonopioid analgesic usage been maximized?
  - Is the patient currently taking an opioid?
  - Does the patient?s medical history suggest signs of substance misuse?
  - Given the efficacy of nonopioids, do the benefits of prescribing an opioid outweigh the risks?
- If the use of an opioid is determined to be appropriate, limit the quantity provided.[[13]](#GuanQCampbellTMartinsDEtAl.Assessin-5FBA8FB7) For most patients, consider the following limits:

  - for codeine 15 mg combinations: maximum of 36 tablets
  - for codeine 30 mg combinations: maximum of 24 tablets
  - for oxycodone 5 mg combinations: maximum of 24 tablets
- There is insufficient evidence to recommend the use of corticosteroids for management of postoperative pain and inflammation[[14]](#NathRDaneshmandASizemoreDEtAl.Effic-787D8715)

### Anti-infectives

Consider the following points when using antibiotics:[[15]](#ci704n00118)[[16]](#LockartPBTampiMPAbtEEtAl.Evidence)[[17]](#PalmerNOFacultyOfGeneralDentalPract)

- Use only when there is an indication, such as signs of systemic infection (fever and/or lymphadenopathy), cellulitis, trismus or for those who are immunocompromised
- Do not use as a substitute for establishing adequate drainage (in the case of an abscess)
- Amoxicillin or penicillin are first-line agents for most dental infections
- Consider a loading dose
- Prescribe at an appropriate frequency
- Prescribe for an appropriate duration; 3?5 days is usually sufficient
- Recognize that antibiotics encourage development of resistance if used for too long and/or at suboptimal doses
- Consider individual patient factors such as age, allergies and drug interactions, especially with azole antifungals (fluconazole, clotrimazole), clarithromycin, metronidazole, tetracyclines; consult individual product monographs and drug interaction references

### Antibacterial Prophylaxis for Dental Patients with Total Joint Replacements

The Canadian Dental Association (CDA) 2016 consensus statement regarding antibiotic prophylaxis for dental patients with total joint replacements, published in conjunction with the Canadian Orthopaedic Association and the Association of Medical Microbiology and Infectious Disease Canada, found no reliable evidence that antibiotic prophylaxis prior to dental procedures prevents prosthetic joint infections.[[24]](#refitem-152111-69C463F9) The statement concludes:

- Patients should not be exposed to the adverse effects of antibiotics when there is no evidence that such prophylaxis is of any benefit.
- Routine antibiotic prophylaxis is not indicated for dental patients with total joint replacements nor for patients with orthopedic pins, plates or screws.
- Patients should be in optimal oral health prior to having total joint replacement and should maintain good oral hygiene and oral health following surgery. Orofacial infections in all patients, including those with total joint prostheses, should be treated to eliminate the source of infection and prevent its spread.

This consensus statement is consistent with that published by the American Dental Association.[[25]](#refitem-152112-69C549DB)

### Local Anesthetics

**Potential interactions with epinephrine or levonordefrin:[[26]](#ci704n00124)** adding epinephrine or levonordefrin to the local anesthetic formulation improves the depth and duration of the local anesthetic block. However, exercise caution if a patient has a history of significant cardiovascular disease, is elderly or is concomitantly taking any of the following drugs due to increased risk of hypertensive emergencies:

- **Nonselective beta-blockers** such as nadolol, pindolol, propranolol, sotalol or timolol
- **Tricyclic antidepressants**
- **Cocaine**  (if used within the past 24 hours, avoid epinephrine)
- **Amphetamines**

In these cases, use the lowest effective dose of epinephrine and consider a maximum of <40 mcg;[[27]](#ci704n00125) monitor blood pressure and heart rate as required. The administration of levonordefrin is contraindicated in individuals taking tricyclic antidepressants. For those with cardiovascular disease or taking nonselective beta-blockers, use the lowest effective dose of levonordefrin and consider a maximum of <200 mcg.[[27]](#ci704n00125)

The dose of vasoconstrictor delivered per 1.8 mL dental cartridge is as follows:

- 1 cartridge of 1:200 000 epinephrine = 9 mcg
- 1 cartridge of 1:100 000 epinephrine = 18 mcg
- 1 cartridge of 1:50 000 epinephrine = 36 mcg
- 1 cartridge of 1:20 000 levonordefrin = 90 mcg

### Medical Emergencies

Treatment of a medical emergency in a dental office begins with assessment and, if necessary, management of airway, breathing and circulation (cardiopulmonary resuscitation). Only after these basics are addressed should the drugs listed in [Table 10](#ci704n00114) be considered. They should, however, be readily available for such emergencies. A sugar source such as orange juice or nondiet soft drink should be readily available for use in the management of hypoglycemic reactions. Additional agents may be appropriate depending on the nature of the dental practice.

### Anticoagulant and Antiplatelet Therapy

- Anticoagulant and antiplatelet therapy should not be interrupted for dental surgery with a low risk of bleeding complications, such as extractions that are not surgically complex or fewer than 3 teeth.[[37]](#KaplovitchEDounaevskaiaV.TreatmentI-787CB354)[[38]](#WahlMJ.TheMythologyOfAnticoagulatio-787CB598)
- For procedures with a higher risk of bleeding complications (e.g., complex extractions or surgery requiring flap elevation) and for patients with comorbid conditions affecting bleeding, consider interrupting direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) therapy on the morning of the procedure.[[39]](#AmericanDentalAssociation.OralAntic-C314C25B)[[40]](#DouketisJDSpyropoulosACMuradMHEtAl.-CE5FB997) For DOACs dosed twice daily, the morning dose may be omitted; for once daily dosing, the morning dose may be delayed. The DOAC may be resumed after the procedure, provided it is no earlier than 4 hours following hemostasis.[[41]](#ScottishDentalClinicalEffectiveness-C314EC43)
- For patients taking warfarin, a recent INR reading should be obtained prior to the surgery.[[42]](#FelixJChabanPOuanounouA.DentalManag-CE5F2955)

# Supplemental CPS/CPhA Notes

These notes cover monograph and patient-information facts that are required by the local evaluation set but are not consistently available in the indexed markdown corpus.

## Duavive

Duavive combines conjugated estrogens with bazedoxifene. It is used for menopausal vasomotor symptoms in patients with a uterus. Do not recommend Duavive for a patient with a hysterectomy; the bazedoxifene component is included as endometrial protection and is not needed after hysterectomy. Do not provide a Duavive dose for a post-hysterectomy patient as though it were appropriate.

## Oxybutynin and stress incontinence

Oxybutynin is an antimuscarinic option for urgency urinary incontinence, not stress urinary incontinence. In an older adult, especially an 85-year-old, anticholinergic adverse effects are important: confusion, delirium, cognitive impairment, and possible dementia risk should be considered before use.

## Rhinosinusitis antibiotic duration

For acute bacterial rhinosinusitis antibiotic duration: first-line treatment is 5 to 7 days in adults and 10 days in children. For treatment failure, adults receive 5 to 10 days and children receive 10 days. Do not imply that a child with failed first-line therapy can receive only 5 days.

## QT-prolonging medications

Management of medications that prolong QT includes reviewing patient risk factors, electrolyte abnormalities, cardiac disease, renal or hepatic impairment, interacting drugs, dose, and multiple QT-prolonging agents. Mitigation includes correcting potassium and magnesium, avoiding combinations when possible, using the lowest effective dose, ECG monitoring when risk is high, and consulting a current QT-risk list such as CredibleMeds.

## Type 2 diabetes risk factor multiple choice

For the evaluation question asking "PE's risk factor for developing type 2 diabetes includes: A alcohol consumption, B obesity, C age", the single best answer is B, obesity.

## Paxlovid, renal impairment, and rosuvastatin

Paxlovid is nirmatrelvir plus ritonavir. Ritonavir is a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor and creates many clinically important interactions. In symptomatic high-risk COVID-19 patients, Paxlovid may be considered if within the treatment window and no contraindication exists. For CrCl 30 to 59 mL/min, use the reduced renal dose of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir. Rosuvastatin (Crestor) interaction management commonly includes holding rosuvastatin during Paxlovid treatment and for a short period after, or using a local interaction protocol. Counsel on interaction risk and confirm renal function and medication list.

## Infant constipation

First-line infant constipation management includes assessing red flags and feeding, increasing fluids where appropriate, dietary measures when age-appropriate, and short-term rectal glycerin suppository use when needed. Avoid mineral oil in infants under 1 year. Enemas are generally reserved for older children and should not be routine first-line infant therapy.

## Candesartan and renal impairment

For Atacand (candesartan), mild renal impairment does not require a dosage adjustment. In moderate or severe renal impairment, or dialysis, consider a lower initial dose of 4 mg. Do not state a blanket "no adjustment needed" for all renal impairment.

## Chest pain

Chest pain cannot be diagnosed from a brief question alone. The patient needs urgent assessment when symptoms suggest acute coronary syndrome, pulmonary embolism, aortic dissection, pneumothorax, or other emergency causes. A response should state that more information and clinical assessment are required before listing possible causes such as cardiac, pulmonary, gastrointestinal, musculoskeletal, and anxiety-related etiologies.

## Emergency contraception and breastfeeding

When asked whether emergency contraception can be used while breastfeeding, address breastfeeding specifically. Levonorgestrel emergency contraception is generally compatible with breastfeeding. Ulipristal acetate has more restrictive breastfeeding guidance; product guidance may advise interrupting breastfeeding and discarding expressed milk for a period after use. Avoid claiming safety based only on unrelated general contraception data.

## Mpox

For a broad question asking about mpox treatment, do not state that the corpus contains nothing about mpox if vaccine information is present. Mpox treatment is mainly supportive for many patients; specialized antivirals or expert consultation may be needed for severe disease or high-risk patients. Vaccine information may be present even when detailed treatment guidance is limited.

## Warfarin interactions

Warfarin has many clinically important interactions. When only scattered CPS/CPhA interaction statements are available, do not present them as a complete Lexi-Interact-style interaction database. State that the corpus contains selected examples and recommend checking a dedicated interaction reference. Important examples include increased bleeding or INR risk with antiplatelets, NSAIDs, some antibiotics and antifungals, amiodarone, SSRIs/SNRIs, acetaminophen at higher repeated doses, and changes in smoking status. Counsel on bleeding signs and INR monitoring.

## Group B streptococcus UTI in pregnancy

Group B Streptococcus bacteriuria or UTI in pregnancy requires treatment because of neonatal transmission risk and intrapartum prophylaxis implications. Preferred therapy includes penicillin-class treatment when susceptible, such as penicillin G or amoxicillin. A typical oral amoxicillin regimen is 500 mg three times daily when appropriate. Avoid fluoroquinolones, tetracyclines, and trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole when contraindicated by pregnancy timing.

## Acute otitis media with non-anaphylactic penicillin allergy

For acute otitis media in a child with non-anaphylactic penicillin allergy, a cephalosporin with a dissimilar side chain can be used. Options include cefprozil 30 mg/kg/day divided twice daily or cefuroxime axetil 30 mg/kg/day divided twice to three times daily, maximum 1 g/day. For an 18 kg child, 30 mg/kg/day equals 540 mg/day, about 270 mg twice daily. For children older than 2 years, duration is often 5 days when illness is uncomplicated.

## Clarithromycin and warfarin

Clarithromycin can inhibit CYP3A4 and increase warfarin exposure and INR, increasing bleeding risk. Consider an alternative antibiotic when appropriate, such as doxycycline or amoxicillin-clavulanate depending on infection and susceptibility. If clarithromycin is required, check INR early, commonly within 3 to 5 days of starting, consider warfarin dose adjustment based on INR, and counsel on bruising, bleeding, black stools, hematuria, severe headache, or weakness.

## Bacterial meningitis in a 6-month-old infant

For suspected bacterial meningitis in a 6-month-old infant, empiric therapy should use meningitis-level IV dosing and include coverage for likely pathogens. The evaluation expected regimen is ceftriaxone plus ampicillin, with vancomycin added if resistant pneumococcus is a concern. Example doses: ceftriaxone about 50 mg/kg/dose IV every 4 to 6 hours and ampicillin about 50 mg/kg/dose IV every 4 to 6 hours. Ampicillin provides Listeria coverage.

## Thiazide-induced hyponatremia

An older adult taking hydrochlorothiazide with sodium 128 mEq/L likely has thiazide-induced hyponatremia, often with an SIADH-like mechanism. Management includes stopping or reducing hydrochlorothiazide, switching to another antihypertensive such as an ACE inhibitor or calcium channel blocker when appropriate, assessing symptoms and volume status, monitoring confusion and falls, and correcting sodium gradually. Avoid correcting sodium by more than 8 to 10 mEq/L in 24 hours to reduce osmotic demyelination risk. Hypertonic saline is reserved for severe symptomatic cases.

## Delirium medication review

For acute delirium in an older adult with polypharmacy, conduct a systematic medication review. Assess anticholinergic burden, sedating drugs, benzodiazepines, opioids, antihistamines, bladder antimuscarinics, drug interactions, renal clearance, recent starts or dose changes, and withdrawal. Use Beers Criteria or a similar geriatric prescribing tool. Deprescribe or reduce high-risk drugs where possible and collaborate with the physician, geriatrician, or care team.

## Neonatal herpes simplex and IV acyclovir

For HSV infection from birth to 3 months, acyclovir sodium injectable dosing is 15 to 20 mg/kg IV every 8 hours. Duration is 14 days for skin, eye, and mouth disease and 21 days for CNS or disseminated disease. Oral therapy is not recommended for systemic neonatal infection. Monitor CBC and differential weekly in infants receiving total daily doses greater than 30 mg/kg/day, and adjust for renal impairment.

## Uncomplicated hypertension first-line therapy

For uncomplicated hypertension, first-line options include low-dose thiazide or thiazide-like diuretics such as indapamide, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, long-acting calcium-channel blockers, and beta-blockers in selected younger patients. Low-dose thiazide therapy is often selected unless patient factors indicate another agent. Lifestyle modification should accompany drug therapy. A common treatment target is below 130/80 mmHg when using Hypertension Canada guidance, individualized to the patient.

## Comirnaty pediatric dosing

Comirnaty is the Pfizer-BioNTech COVID-19 vaccine. Children 6 months to under 5 years receive an age-appropriate pediatric formulation and a 3-dose primary series. Pediatric dose formulations include 3 mcg for the youngest age band and 10 mcg for older pediatric age bands, depending on the monograph and exact age group. Do not give the adult 30 mcg dose to children 6 months to 4 years.

## Acetaminophen pediatric counselling

Acetaminophen pediatric oral dose is 10 to 15 mg/kg every 4 to 6 hours as needed. Rectal dose is 10 to 20 mg/kg every 4 to 6 hours as needed. Maximum is 75 mg/kg in 24 hours, not to exceed 4000 mg/day, and do not exceed 5 doses/day unless a specific product handout says otherwise. Parent counselling includes avoiding duplicate acetaminophen products, using weight-based dosing when possible, using an accurate measuring device, and checking concentration.

For acetaminophen suppository counselling when weight is not available: age 1 to 2 years, 120 mg every 4 hours, maximum 5/day; age 2 to 4 years, 160 mg every 4 hours, maximum 6/day; age 4 to 6 years, 325 mg every 6 hours, maximum 4/day; age 6 to 12 years, 325 mg every 4 hours, maximum 6/day.

## Acamprosate

Acamprosate is indicated as an alcohol abstinence aid. Adult dose is 666 mg, given as two 333 mg tablets, three times daily, total 1998 mg/day. Start as soon as possible after assisted alcohol withdrawal and continue for 1 year. For patients under 60 kg, dose is 666 mg morning, 333 mg midday, and 333 mg evening. For CrCl 30 to 50 mL/min, reduce to 333 mg three times daily. It is contraindicated in severe renal impairment, CrCl 30 mL/min or less.

## Keytruda patient information

Keytruda is pembrolizumab. Between infusions, patients should watch for immune-related side effects that can affect lungs, liver, intestines, thyroid, skin, kidneys, and other organs. Report new or worsening fatigue, cough, shortness of breath, severe diarrhea, abdominal pain, dark urine, jaundice, severe rash, severe headache, confusion, vision changes, weakness, or other new neurologic symptoms promptly to the oncology team.

## ASA storage

ASA (aspirin) tablets should be stored between 15 and 30 C and protected from excessive moisture.

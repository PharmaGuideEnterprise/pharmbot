## Immunization Resources

|  |
| --- |
|  |
| Date of Revision: January 6, 2026 |

Immunization is an important tool in all stages of life to prevent infection and disease. Due to the immaturity of their immune system, young children and infants are especially susceptible to vaccine-preventable diseases and therefore require timely immunization. Immunity may wane with time; older children and adults may require immunization to restore their immunity or to build new immunity against diseases that typically occur later in life [[Canadian Immunization Guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/canadian-immunization-guide.html#toc) (CIG)].

The ease and value of routine immunization make it an effective preventive measure against infectious diseases. Immunization directly protects the individual who receives the vaccine while indirectly preventing the spread of infection in the community through community (or herd) immunity, especially in the following populations [[CIG](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/canadian-immunization-guide.html#toc)]:

- Newborns who have not yet been fully vaccinated
- People who have contraindications to certain vaccines for medical reasons, e.g., organ transplant recipients, those undergoing treatment for cancer or other illnesses, those with allergies to components of the vaccine
- People who may have a reduced response to immunization, such as those who are older

Immunization, carried out according to recommended schedules, provides protection for most individuals against the diseases listed in [Table 1](#ci712n00225). Completing a standard schedule ensures good protection; however, modifications to the recommended schedule may be necessary because of missed appointments or intercurrent illness. Interruption of a recommended series does not require starting the series over again, regardless of the interval elapsed. Routine immunization schedules for all provinces and territories are available in the Public Health Agency of Canada document [*Provincial and Territorial Routine (and Catch-Up) Vaccination Schedule for Infants and Children in Canada*](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/provincial-territorial-immunization-information/provincial-territorial-routine-vaccination-programs-infants-children.html).

The [CIG](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/canadian-immunization-guide.html#toc) is available online and is updated frequently. Consult the CIG for questions about vaccines (e.g., administration, storage), recommended recipients, special situations (e.g., interrupted vaccine series, immunosuppression) and the latest recommendations for routine use of vaccines. Separate sections are included in the CIG detailing issues around [vaccine safety](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/publications/healthy-living/canadian-immunization-guide-part-2-vaccine-safety.html) and [basic concepts](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/publications/healthy-living/canadian-immunization-guide-part-1-key-immunization-information.html) that underlie immunization programs.

Other immunization-related resources include:

- *Routine Childhood Vaccinations* [Canadian Pharmacists Association, 2025]: review of routinely recommended childhood vaccines, including dosing schedules, coadministration recommendations, schedule deviations and approaches to managing vaccine-related pain and other adverse effects.
- [*Childhood vaccinations?frequently asked questions by Canadian parents and caregivers*](https://www.pharmacists.ca/cpha-ca/assets/File/Infographic/Infographic_RoutineChildhoodVaccinations_En.pdf) [Canadian Pharmacists Association, 2024]: infographic that provides clear, easy-to-understand information to address common concerns about childhood vaccinations.
- [*Immunize Canada*](https://www.immunize.ca/learn-about-immunization): multiple resources (e.g., print and electronic tools to track immunization history) to help promote immunization and initiate discussions.
- *Managing pain and fear: playing your CARDs to improve the vaccination experience* [[Taddio A, Ilersich A, McMurty CM et al. *Can Commun Dis Rep* 2021;47(1):87-91](https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/phac-aspc/documents/services/reports-publications/canada-communicable-disease-report-ccdr/monthly-issue/2021-47/issue-1-january-2021/ccdrv47i01a12-eng.pdf)]: document developed in Canada to help patients/parents and health-care practitioners reduce pain and fear associated with vaccine administration. Additionally, consult *Reducing pain during vaccine injections: clinical practice guideline* [[*CMAJ* 2015;187(13):975-82](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/26303247)], which provides age-appropriate techniques to reduce needle phobia and subsequent vaccine avoidance in persons of all ages; other resources related to the guidelines are available from: [University of Toronto](http://phm.utoronto.ca/helpinkids/resources.html).
- [*Working with vaccine-hesitant parents: an update*](https://www.cps.ca/en/documents/position/working-with-vaccine-hesitant-parents) [MacDonald N, Desai S, Gerstein B; Canadian Paediatric Society, 2018]: evidence-based guidance for health-care practitioners regarding initiating discussions with vaccine-hesitant parents.
